CRM Assistant

AUTHOR: NIPUNA TENNAKOON STATUS: BUILT ON SYNTHETIC DATA 2026 VOLUNTEER ENGAGEMENT

01 The problem

Staff spend a large share of their day on repetitive work: looking up student records, writing routine emails, and updating fields by hand. Information about any one student is spread across records, notes, and documents, so answering even a simple question means digging through the system manually. This is slow, and it means students who need attention can slip through unnoticed.

02 The solution

An assistant built into the CRM that staff operate in plain English. A staff member types what they need, and the assistant retrieves the right records, summarises a student's history, drafts a message, flags students at risk of dropping out, or updates a record on request. It runs entirely on local hardware, so student data never leaves the office.

03 Objectives

  • Cut the time staff spend on manual lookups and routine writing
  • Surface at-risk students and cooling leads earlier, before they are lost
  • Keep all student data on the organisation's own machines
  • Stay reliable and accurate enough to be trusted in daily use

04 Who it is for

Front-desk, admin, and student-support staff. The people using it are not technical, so every interaction happens in plain language with no query syntax or forms to learn.

05 Scope

In scope

  • Five staff actions: query, summarise, draft, flag, update
  • A small predictive model for at-risk detection
  • An evaluation harness to measure accuracy
  • A realistic mock CRM database to build against

Out of scope (for now)

  • Live production data on day one
  • Sending emails automatically without human review
  • Document scanning and image reading
  • Changes to the existing CRM's core software

06 Success criteria

  • Common lookups can be completed in a single plain-English request
  • At-risk flags line up with what actually happened in past records
  • Measured accuracy on a fixed test set stays above an agreed threshold
  • The whole system runs comfortably within 24GB of memory on local hardware

07 Phased plan

PHASE 1

Retrieval and Q&A

Staff can ask questions and get answers pulled from records and policy documents.

PHASE 2

Actions and predictions

Add summarising, email drafting, record updates, and the at-risk model.

PHASE 3

Evaluation and hardening

Build the accuracy test harness and tighten reliability before wider use.

08 Technology

PythonFastAPIChromaDB sentence-transformersQwen3-14B (LM Studio / Ollama) FastMCPSQLitescikit-learn

As built: Python, FastAPI, SQLite (with FTS5 search), Qwen3-14B and nomic embeddings in LM Studio, scikit-learn for training only, and a React staff app. ChromaDB, sentence-transformers and FastMCP turned out not to be needed; the technical design (document 2, section 10) says why.

09 Where it stands (October 2026)

All three phases are built and tested against a synthetic college database of 600 students. Against the success criteria:

  • Single-request lookups: yes. Lookups, summaries, drafts, at-risk lists and changes each take one plain-English message, typically 6-17 seconds on the local model.
  • At-risk flags match the past: on the synthetic records, the trained model ranks students better than the old hand-written score (AUC 0.886 vs 0.838) and catches about half of withdrawals at its flag line. This needs re-checking on real records before anyone relies on it.
  • Accuracy above an agreed threshold: the threshold is 95% of 79 test cases, every safety and update case, and no records written. The latest run passed it.
  • Runs within 24GB: 13.9 GB peak during a full test run.

Still to do before staff use it: connect the college's real records, run it as a service on the office network with https and nightly backups.
