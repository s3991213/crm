# Deferral, Suspension and Cancellation Policy

## Deferring a course
A student can ask to defer the start of their course, or to take a break during it, because of compassionate or compelling circumstances. Examples are serious illness (with a medical certificate), the death or serious illness of a close family member, a natural disaster or political upheaval at home, or a traumatic experience. Work commitments and holidays are not normally accepted.

## How long a deferral can be
A deferral is approved for up to 6 months at a time, and no more than 12 months in total over the whole course. The student must agree a return date. If they don't come back on that date, Student Services contacts them, and their enrolment may be cancelled.

## Applying for a deferral
The student fills in the deferral form and attaches evidence. Student Services decides within 10 working days and tells the student in writing. For international students the deferral is reported in PRISMS, and it may affect their visa, so they are told to check with the Department of Home Affairs.

## Suspension or cancellation by the college
The college may suspend or cancel an enrolment for serious misconduct, unpaid fees (more than 28 days overdue with no payment plan), or not returning after a deferral. The student is first sent a notice of intention, and has 20 working days to use the complaints and appeals process. The enrolment isn't changed until that time has passed or the appeal is finished, except where the student's health or safety, or other people's, is at risk.

## Withdrawing from a course
A student who wants to leave fills in the withdrawal form. Their counsellor offers a meeting first, to check whether support, a payment plan or a deferral would help. Refunds follow the fees and refunds policy.
