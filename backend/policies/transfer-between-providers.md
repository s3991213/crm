# Transfer Between Providers Policy

## The six-month rule
International students may not transfer to another college before they have completed 6 months of their main (principal) course, unless this college agrees and gives them a release letter. After 6 months, no release letter is needed.

## When a release letter is given
The college gives a release letter when the transfer is in the student's best interest, for example:
- the course isn't right for the student, and they have evidence of that (such as advice from a counsellor);
- compassionate or compelling circumstances;
- the college hasn't delivered the course as promised;
- the student has been refused a place at a higher level and can show an offer from another provider for a suitable course.

A release letter is not usually given when the student's attendance or course progress is unsatisfactory and they haven't taken part in the intervention offered, or when the request is only to pay lower fees elsewhere.

## Asking for a release letter
The student fills in the transfer request form and attaches a valid letter of offer from the new provider. The college decides within 10 working days. There is no charge for a release letter.

## If the request is refused
The student is told why in writing, and has 20 working days to appeal through the complaints and appeals process. The refusal isn't reported in PRISMS until the appeal is finished.
